The Language

An ancient tongue at the roof of the world.

Wakhi — or X̌ik-wor as its speakers call it — is an Eastern Iranian language over a thousand years old, spoken in the high-altitude valleys where Pakistan, Afghanistan, Tajikistan, and China converge. It is the voice of the Pamir mountains.

Classified as endangered, Wakhi has never had a standardized digital writing system that its speakers could use across borders. Academics have documented it in Latin script since 1876. The community types it informally today — but without the right tools.

"Wakhi writing is already increasingly appearing online and in social media using Roman letters."

— Endangered Language Alliance, New York

This keyboard changes that. No ads. No data collection. Just a free, open-source tool so that a Wakhi speaker in Gojal, Wakhan, Gorno-Badakhshan, or Xinjiang can type their language on any Android phone.
